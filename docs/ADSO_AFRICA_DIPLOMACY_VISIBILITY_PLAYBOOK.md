# ADSO AFRICA — Diplomatie, Visibilité & Institutional Outreach Playbook

## Mission

ADSO AFRICA doit être visible sans se comporter comme une institution publique. Sa diplomatie consiste à créer des passerelles sérieuses entre NDSA, les acteurs publics, les établissements, les entreprises, les ONG et les partenaires de développement.

## Institutional thesis

Africa is facing an urgent road-safety implementation challenge. In April 2026, an AU high-level ministerial panel called for accelerated action, highlighting data systems, stronger implementation, protection of vulnerable road users, capacity and collaboration. The AU also describes AfRSO as a continental platform for harmonized data, policy coordination and practical technical support. ADSO AFRICA should position itself as a **complementary technology and learning layer**, never as a substitute for AfRSO or national authorities.

## The diplomatic posture

ADSO AFRICA speaks with four principles:

1. **Respect sovereignty** — national rules and institutions remain authoritative.
2. **Offer before asking** — bring a concrete demonstration, diagnostic or pilot concept.
3. **Evidence before claims** — never invent impact, partnerships or endorsements.
4. **Co-construction** — ask institutions where their existing programs have gaps that a digital learning layer could complement.

## The 30-second institutional introduction

> « ADSO AFRICA est une plateforme technologique africaine qui aide à éduquer, former et évaluer les usagers de la route — pas seulement les conducteurs. Nous travaillons notamment avec les jeunes, les apprentis de tous secteurs, les piétons, les passagers, les motocyclistes et les conducteurs. Notre approche combine scénarios immersifs, compétences, contenus adaptés aux pays et mesure des résultats d'apprentissage. Nous cherchons d'abord à construire des pilotes utiles avec les acteurs déjà engagés sur le terrain. »

## The institutional conversation

Never begin with a sales pitch. Ask:

- Quelles populations sont aujourd'hui les plus difficiles à atteindre ?
- Quels publics vulnérables sont insuffisamment couverts par les programmes existants ?
- Comment les jeunes et les apprentis sont-ils actuellement sensibilisés ?
- Quels indicateurs de formation sont disponibles ?
- Où se trouvent les difficultés de suivi et d'évaluation ?
- Quelles formations pourraient être digitalisées ou renforcées par la simulation ?
- Quels contenus doivent absolument rester nationaux et validés localement ?
- Quel serait un pilote réaliste sur 60–90 jours ?

## The pilot offer

Propose a small, measurable pilot instead of a national procurement request.

### ADSO Africa Vulnerable Mobility Pilot

Target example:

- 100–500 learners;
- apprentices and/or young road users;
- one defined institution, training centre, company or community;
- one or two risk themes;
- baseline assessment;
- immersive learning;
- post-training assessment;
- anonymized/aggregated report;
- lessons learned;
- scale recommendation.

The pilot is a proof of learning and operational value, not proof of reduced road deaths unless a scientifically appropriate evaluation is designed.

## Visibility engine

### 1. Institutional landing page

Create a dedicated route/content surface for:

`/institutions`

It should answer in less than two minutes:

- What problem?
- For whom?
- What does ADSO do?
- What can an institution deploy?
- What can it measure?
- How does a pilot work?
- How can an institution contact NDSA?

### 2. Institutional one-pager

Produce a concise document with:

- mission;
- problem;
- solution;
- vulnerable-user focus;
- apprentice focus;
- immersive demonstration;
- institutional cockpit;
- country packs;
- pilot methodology;
- contact path.

### 3. Executive demo

The demo path must be:

`Problem → vulnerable person → decision → consequence → learning → competency → institutional insight`

Do not lead with dashboards. Lead with a human problem and then show the evidence layer.

### 4. Proof library

Build a repository of reusable, verified assets:

- screenshots;
- short immersive clips;
- anonymized pilot metrics;
- case studies;
- methodology notes;
- Country Pack examples;
- institutional FAQ;
- product fact sheet.

Every asset needs a status:

`DEMO / PILOT / VERIFIED / PUBLIC`

### 5. Thought leadership

Publish useful material rather than advertising:

- « Pourquoi l'apprenti de tous secteurs est aussi un usager vulnérable »
- « Former à la mobilité, pas seulement à la conduite »
- « Comment mesurer l'acquisition de compétences de mobilité »
- « Digitaliser la prévention routière en contexte de faible connectivité »
- « Country Packs : adapter une plateforme panafricaine aux réalités nationales »

### 6. Institutional relationship CRM

Track every relationship with:

- institution;
- country;
- sector;
- contact role;
- first contact;
- current stage;
- need identified;
- proposed pilot;
- next action;
- owner;
- documents sent;
- meeting notes;
- evidence/status;
- follow-up date.

Do not scrape or collect personal data without a lawful and appropriate basis.

## Mali first

Use Mali as the first physical laboratory because the founder is currently there. The objective is not to claim institutional endorsement. The objective is to identify one credible partner and run one useful pilot.

Suggested route:

`NDSA → ADSO AFRICA demo → local stakeholder interview → small pilot → evidence → institutional case study → regional expansion`

## Benin second reference

Because the founder is Beninese, Benin can become a second reference terrain after the Mali proof point. The narrative should remain continental:

**« Conçu en Afrique, testé sur des réalités africaines, adaptable à chaque pays. »**

Avoid making nationality the product's institutional identity.

## The diplomatic sequence

### Before meeting

Research the institution's mandate, current strategy, programs and public priorities.

Prepare one relevant ADSO scenario and one pilot proposition.

### During meeting

Listen 70%, present 30%.

Identify one concrete problem ADSO could help address.

Do not oversell.

### After meeting

Send:

1. thank-you note;
2. one-page concept;
3. requested evidence;
4. proposed pilot scope;
5. next meeting objective.

### Follow-up rhythm

Day 0 — meeting
Day 2 — concise follow-up
Day 7 — useful resource
Day 14 — pilot refinement
Day 30 — decision checkpoint

Never spam.

## The institutional narrative

ADSO AFRICA should be introduced through this sequence:

**The human problem**
→ vulnerable road users are heavily exposed.

**The learning gap**
→ prevention often needs stronger reach, contextualization and measurable learning.

**The technology layer**
→ ADSO combines immersive scenarios, learning, competencies and analytics.

**The African advantage**
→ country packs, local contexts, languages and low-bandwidth design.

**The institutional value**
→ deploy, measure, learn, improve.

**The pilot**
→ small, measurable, co-designed.

## Evidence discipline

Current continental evidence supports the urgency of vulnerable-road-user protection and data/capacity strengthening. The April 2026 AU panel explicitly highlighted protection of vulnerable road users, data systems, capacity and implementation; AfRSO's 2026–2027 work also emphasizes harmonized data and institutional capacity. WHO reports that vulnerable road users account for about half of road traffic fatalities in the African Region. These facts support ADSO's strategic relevance, but they do not constitute endorsement of ADSO.

## Success definition

The visibility mechanism is successful when it generates:

- qualified institutional conversations;
- pilot invitations;
- technical evaluations;
- demonstrations;
- letters of interest;
- co-designed pilots;
- verified case studies;
- repeat deployments;
- references across countries.

Vanity metrics such as followers alone are secondary.

## Diplomatic north star

**ADSO AFRICA should become known not because it says it is African, but because institutions recognize that it understands African mobility realities and can turn prevention and education into deployable, measurable learning.**

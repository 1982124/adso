# ADSO AFRICA — Institutional Product Blueprint

## Purpose

Turn ADSO AFRICA into an institutional-grade African platform for education, prevention, mobility skills and evidence-oriented learning intelligence.

## Core proposition

ADSO AFRICA helps organizations reach vulnerable road users and mobility populations with contextualized education, immersive scenarios, competency assessment and measurable learning outcomes.

## Priority population: apprentices

An apprentice is **not automatically a driver**.

An apprentice from any sector may be exposed to traffic risk while walking, cycling, riding a motorcycle, taking public/shared transport, travelling as a passenger, working near roads or occasionally driving as part of work.

ADSO must therefore support occupation-aware but mobility-independent safety pathways.

### Apprentice profile model

`OCCUPATION → MOBILITY MODES → RISK EXPOSURES → SAFETY SKILLS → ASSESSMENT → PROGRESSION`

Examples of occupation contexts:

- construction and building trades;
- mechanics and vehicle trades;
- agriculture;
- commerce;
- hospitality;
- services;
- industry;
- logistics;
- crafts;
- other vocational learning pathways.

The occupation list must remain extensible and must not imply that ADSO certifies professional competence in the occupation itself.

## Institutional modules

### 1. ADSO Learn
Courses and learning paths.

### 2. ADSO Immersive
Realistic decision-based traffic scenarios.

### 3. ADSO Skills
Competency framework and progression.

### 4. ADSO Country
Country-specific legal, linguistic and contextual content.

### 5. ADSO Institutional Cockpit
Organizations manage cohorts, assign learning, monitor progress and export reports.

### 6. ADSO Data
Aggregated learning intelligence, with privacy-by-design.

### 7. ADSO Impact
Before/after measurement of learning and intervention outcomes.

### 8. ADSO AI
Teacher, Coach, Examiner, Simulator, Road Safety Analyst, Business Analyst and Support agents.

## Institutional dashboard principles

The cockpit must show real data only.

Recommended indicators:

- enrolled population;
- active learners;
- completion rate;
- competency acquisition;
- recurrent errors;
- risk-recognition performance;
- scenario performance;
- results by cohort;
- results by location when lawful and meaningful;
- intervention participation;
- before/after learning measures.

Do not infer accident reduction from these indicators without a validated evaluation design.

## Institutional deployment model

`DIAGNOSTIC → COHORT → TRAINING → IMMERSION → ASSESSMENT → CORRECTION → REASSESSMENT → REPORT`

## Pilot strategy

Mali is the first proof-of-concept terrain.

The pilot must demonstrate a real, measurable use case with a defined population and documented learning outcomes.

Benin is a natural second reference terrain because the platform's founder is Beninese, but the product must remain neutral, continental and reusable across countries.

## Institutional readiness checklist

- role-based access;
- organization isolation;
- consent and privacy controls;
- exportable reports;
- auditable content versions;
- Country Pack provenance;
- offline/low-bandwidth strategy;
- multilingual UX;
- stable APIs;
- monitoring and error tracking;
- security review;
- end-to-end testing.

## Strategic moat

The defensible combination is:

`AFRICA-NATIVE + COUNTRY PACKS + VULNERABLE-USER LEARNING + IMMERSIVE SCENARIOS + SKILLS INTELLIGENCE + INSTITUTIONAL COCKPIT + IMPACT MEASUREMENT + AI + OFFLINE`

This is the target architecture, not a claim that every component is already fully implemented in production.
